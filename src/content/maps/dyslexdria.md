## The Map

Dyslexdria is a fantasy map that feels familiar only long enough to mislead you. At 3308×1440 with 44.2% land, it has the scale and proportions of a world map, but the geography is rearranged into a strange, fractured puzzle. The name is a warning: do not trust your usual mental atlas.

Its 82 nations make the opening busy, with 69 of them represented by coordinate-based spawn overlays. There are enough recognizable shapes to invite old habits, but the actual routes, gaps, and chokepoints need to be learned on their own terms.

## The Battlefield

### Terrain Overview
Dyslexdria is a high-density macro map. It mixes large landmasses, awkward seas, and many spawn points, so the first phase is about discovering which borders are real threats and which only look close.

### Best Spawns
- **Large interior landmasses** - strong economies if you can simplify your borders.
- **Coastal hinge zones** - good for players who can pivot between land and sea.
- **Island-adjacent starts with mainland access** - flexible without being isolated.

### Avoid
- **Assuming geography behaves like Earth** - familiar shapes can have unfamiliar routes.
- **Crowded chokepoints** - 82 nations means local mistakes become public opportunities quickly.
- **Pure naval openings** - water matters, but the 44.2% land share still leaves armies deciding much of the map.

### Strategic Insights
The key to Dyslexdria is relearning scale. Do not fight because two areas look historically connected; fight because the map's actual channels, distances, and land bridges make the war efficient. Players who adapt fastest turn the confusion into a scouting advantage.
